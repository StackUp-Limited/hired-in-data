1

Product & business cases

The heart of most data science loops. They rarely want the "right" answer - they want to watch you structure an ambiguous problem, pick metrics, and design a way to measure it.

Your PM wants to increase the radius of restaurants shown to a user from 5 miles to 10.
  • What are the pros and cons of the change?
  • How would you help the team decide if it's a good idea?
  • If you only rolled it out to selected restaurants, geos, or times, what would your selection criteria be? (Think: which cuisines travel well, urban vs rural, peak vs off-peak, new vs returning users.)
There's a 10% drop in the share of orders placed in the LA region. How would you investigate it?

Be ready for the interviewer to shift the problem from the customer side to the delivery side mid-question - map both the customer funnel and the delivery funnel.

DoorDash launched a monthly subscription product. How would you measure whether it is performing well? And separately: you see a sudden drop in order completion rates - how do you troubleshoot it?
A case built entirely around ETA (estimated time of arrival).
  • What factors affect ETA? Consider driver behaviour, rider behaviour, and the platform itself.
  • Why does the PM care about ETA?
  • If ETA and ride conversion are positively correlated, how do you interpret that? (It's counterintuitive - longer ETAs usually mean lower conversion, so what special cases flip it?)
  • How would you design an experiment to test a change to ETA, and how would you choose the switchback window? Pros and cons of a short vs long window?
We're launching a promo: "Get 20% off when you spend $40 or more."
  • Why would we launch this, and what metrics evaluate its effectiveness?
  • How would you determine the sample size for the study?
  • If the PM sets the minimum detectable effect (MDE) at 0.5%, how do you interpret that, and how does it affect sample size?
  • You observe a 0.3% effect that is not statistically significant. How do you interpret it, and how do you communicate it to the PM?
You're changing the upfront tipping options from 10 / 15 / 20% to 15 / 20 / 25%. What are the reasons for doing that, how would you evaluate the impact, what metrics would you care about, and how would you set up the experiment? (Remember drivers accept deliveries based on expected payout.)
Meta has two ad types: direct response and brand. A PM thinks brand ad revenue is down. Figure out whether that's true, and why.

The real case often lives in the subtext of the first sentence - here, brand was underperforming relative to direct response, both internally and against industry benchmarks. Listen for the implied comparison.

How would you evaluate user interest in a group video-call feature without running an experiment?
Slack wants to enter a new market. How would you help the team decide which geography to enter?
How would you measure the success of a new credit card launch? (Think primary vs secondary metrics, leading vs lagging indicators.)
A credit card's approval rate dropped 15% last month. How would you investigate the root cause?
A digital-check-deposit app costs X to build and Y a year to maintain. Branch ATM deposits cost the same to build and maintain, plus $0.01 per deposit. How many deposits a year does the app need to break even?
2

Machine learning, stats & modelling

Fundamentals they expect you to explain cleanly, out loud, without notes.

Explain the difference between MAE and MSE. When would you prefer each?
Why might AUC be a better metric than accuracy, and when is that especially true?
What is an LSTM, and what problem with traditional RNNs does it solve?
What causes vanishing and exploding gradients, and how do you address each?
For video recommendation, what's the difference between using a CNN and a Transformer?
You find the single most important feature isn't statistically significant. Give two reasons why that can happen.
Explain multicollinearity, and why it's a problem.
Give two ways to handle an imbalanced dataset, and the implications of one of them.
The search bar currently supports general retail. You now also need it to serve Amazon Fresh searches at the same time. How would you design it?
3

SQL & coding

Write these out fully. The SQL prompts come with the exact columns they gave candidates.

Two SQL questions on the same product - a video-call feature (group calls not yet possible):
  • Return the count of users who made more than 3 video calls to different recipients in the last 7 days. Columns: caller_id | recipient_id | call_id | date
  • What percentage of yesterday's daily active users from France performed a video call? Columns: user_id | country | DAU | date
Write queries to: (1) find the average quantity of each product purchased per transaction each year; (2) get the total amount spent on each item by users who registered in 2022.
Data-structures round:
  • Return the largest positive number that can be formed by rearranging the digits of another positive number.
  • Given a list of meetings (start hour, end hour, audience size), return the largest total number of people attending simultaneously over a continuous period.
  • Given shelf capacities and book sizes (one book per shelf), determine whether all the books fit.
Coding topics to drill: graphs, dynamic programming, binary search, recursion, hashmaps, sliding window, heaps. Their advice: 150+ Leetcode mediums, and know your language's best practices cold.

System design & data modelling

Senior and data-engineering loops. They want a structured design and the trade-offs behind each choice.

Design a ride-sharing database schema.

A reported twist in a final loop: a new carpool feature had been introduced, and the candidate had to reason about how to measure its impact on engagement and revenue.

Design a smart parking system for a busy urban area, with automated slotting via a mobile app. Requirements: real-time occupancy tracking, in-app booking with dynamic pricing, support for an analytics platform, advance booking, and a price fixed at the moment of booking.
Design a notification system.
Design a WhatsApp-group-style app where users share restaurants and venues to vote on and organise events around.
(L5+) Design an ML pipeline. How would you scale a recommendation system, and optimise a model for latency and scalability?
5

Recruiter & hiring-manager screens

Before any of the above, you'll face a screen. Across companies, the same threads come up again and again - have crisp answers ready.

The end-to-end project deep dive - the single most common thread in every screen:
  • Walk me through an ML project you led end-to-end, from idea to deployment.
  • What was the business problem, and why was ML the right solution?
  • What impact were you aiming for, and how did you measure success?
  • How did you define risk levels and decision thresholds?
  • How did you know the new model was better than what existed before?
  • How did you handle concept drift, delayed ground truth, or external changes?
  • How did you explain the results to non-technical stakeholders?
  • What did you use for deployment and orchestration (Airflow, CI/CD, feature store)?
  • How did you collaborate with engineering, compliance, and other cross-functional teams?
Common
Why this company, and why this role in particular? What's your ideal next role, and what are you looking for in your next challenge?
Common
Why did you leave your previous company? When are you looking to start, and what are your salary expectations?
How do you prioritise conflicting deadlines? Tell me about a time you worked in a group - what was your role? Tell me about a conflict or new problem and how you handled it.
Walk me through a project where you used at least two of SQL, Python and Tableau - what was the problem, and what did you use each for? When presenting to leadership, how did you make the key takeaways easy to grasp?
6

How to actually use this bank

A list of questions you skim is worthless. A list you practise out loud is a cheat code.

  • Work each prompt out loud, end to end, before checking anything
  • For any troubleshooting case, draw the funnel - both sides of the marketplace
  • Clarify the goal and the constraints before you start solving
  • For every product case, name your metrics before you design the experiment
  • Know your own projects cold - most screens are just your resume, pressure-tested
  • Prepare crisp answers to "why this company", "why now", and salary expectations

These are real reported questions, not a syllabus. Use them to rehearse how you think, not to memorise answers - interviewers can tell the difference instantly.

Pick five and rehearse them today

Don't read all forty in one sitting and feel prepared. Choose five that match your target role, and answer each one out loud until it's smooth.

The candidates who get offers are the ones who practised the exact questions - said out loud, more than once.
