Free Cheat Sheet

Fifteen questions unicorn data teams are asking right now.

Three each from Stripe, Databricks, Revolut, Discord and Brex, from our community and recent interview reports. Five private companies worth over a billion dollars, all hiring data people, and almost nobody prepares for them.

Compiled by Mandy Liu - ex-Meta Data Scientist, founder of Hired in Data. 50+ data professionals placed, averaging a $54K salary increase.

Stripe

Everything here is money moving. Fraud, lending and payment behaviour, and every question has a cost on the other side of it that you are expected to notice.

Product senseMedium

You have a new fraud model ready to replace the one in production. How would you know it is better, without quietly blocking payments that were fine?

  • Say what "better" means here in numbers, not adjectives.
  • Name the metric that would catch you strangling good transactions to make the fraud number look good.
  • Say how you would compare the two models on the same traffic.

What they're testingWhether you can hold catching fraud and letting good payments through in one answer.

HintAny fraud model looks brilliant if you block enough. The second half of the question is the whole question.

ModellingHard

Stripe wants to lend money to small businesses. All you have to go on is what those businesses have already processed through Stripe. How would you work out who is safe to lend to?

  • Say which signals in payment history actually predict a business failing to repay.
  • Say what you would do about businesses that have only been on the platform a few months.
  • Say how you would know the model is wrong before the defaults arrive.

What they're testingTurning transaction history into a risk model, and knowing what that history cannot see.

HintYou only ever find out what happened to the businesses you said yes to. Say how you would handle that before they ask.

SQLMedium

Find the users whose transaction rate has fallen off a cliff in the last 30 days, compared with what is normal for them.

  • Build "normal for them" from their own history, not the platform average.
  • Decide what counts as a sudden drop, and say why that threshold.
  • Return the user, both rates and the size of the fall, worst first.

What they're testingWindow functions, and a threshold you can defend out loud.

HintComparing each user against themselves is the point. A query that compares everyone to one global average answers a different question.

Databricks

They sell the plumbing, so they ask about the plumbing. Expect to design a system rather than fit a model, and expect the follow-ups to be about what happens when it breaks.

System designHard

Every team builds its own features, so the same thing gets defined two different ways and models behave differently in training than they do in production. Design one shared feature platform for all of them.

  • Cover both jobs: building training sets offline, and serving features fast enough for a live request.
  • Say how a feature's definition stays identical on both sides.
  • Say what happens with drift, with backfills, and with a pipeline that fails at 3am.

What you're givenThe platform has to serve search retrieval, ranking, fraud detection and personalised recommendations. Today each team computes its own features. The result is inconsistent definitions, slow experiments, and training and serving that disagree.

What they're testingWhether you can design a system, not just a model.

HintTraining and serving disagreeing is the named enemy. If your design does not end up with one place where a feature is defined, you have not solved it.

SQLMedium

Explain how you would build a cohort retention table in SQL. Not the exact query - the design.

  • Say how you fix each user's cohort date.
  • Say how you decide someone counts as retained in a later period.
  • Handle the awkward bits: several events in one period, gaps in activity, and a current period that has not finished yet.

What they're testingWhether you have built one of these, or only read about one.

HintThe half-finished current month is where most answers fall over. Say what you would do with it before you are asked.

BehaviouralHard

Tell me about a time a senior leader wanted to go one way on an important engineering decision and you thought they were wrong. What did you do, and what happened after?

What they're testingWhether you can disagree with someone senior and still be a person they want in the room.

Hint"What happened after" is the graded part. A story where you were right and nothing changed scores badly.

Revolut

The hardest technical bar on this sheet. They will ask you to derive things from first principles, and the coding round uses their own problem - money moving between accounts.

Machine learningHard

Derive cross-entropy loss from first principles, then explain what gradient descent does with it.

What they're testingWhether you know the maths behind a line you have typed a hundred times.

HintThey want the derivation, spoken, without notes. "It's the log loss one" is not an answer. This is a question you rehearse, not one you reason your way through on the day.

SQLHard

The same payment sometimes lands twice. Write the query that finds transactions which look like duplicates of each other inside a five-minute window.

  • Say what makes two rows the same payment rather than two real payments.
  • Use window functions to compare each transaction against the one before it.
  • Handle three or more in a row, not just pairs.

What they're testingWindow functions against a fuzzy definition you have to pin down yourself.

HintThe hard part is not the SQL, it is deciding what counts as a duplicate. State your rule before you write a line.

CodingMedium

Money moves between accounts. Write something that spots when it goes round in a circle and ends up back where it started.

What they're testingGraph traversal, and whether you notice why a bank would care.

HintIt is cycle detection in a directed graph. It is also how you find money being passed round a loop to look like real activity - say that out loud, it is half the answer.

Discord

Consumer product work: define a metric for something new, explain a number that moved, and know when a winning test is not worth shipping.

Product senseMedium

Discord has just launched a new voice channel feature. How would you tell whether it worked?

  • Pick one headline metric, and say which smaller ones sit under it.
  • Say how you would tell genuinely new use apart from people moving off text chat.
  • Say what would make you call it a failure.

What they're testingMetric design on a feature where time spent is easily mistaken for value.

HintMinutes in voice is the obvious metric and the trap. Someone sitting alone in an empty channel is counted too.

Product diagnosisMedium

Server joins are down 15% on last week. Find out why.

  • Split it before you theorise: platform, region, new against returning users, and what shipped that week.
  • Say how you would rule out something broken in the data itself.
  • Say what evidence would let you stop and call it.

What you're givenDaily active server joins, down 15% week on week. Nothing else - the rest you have to ask for.

What they're testingA structured search, rather than a list of guesses.

HintCheck the number is real before you explain it. A logging change looks exactly like a 15% drop.

ExperimentationHard

Give me a case where the test came back significant and positive, and you would still say do not ship it.

What they're testingWhether you treat a p-value as evidence or as a decision.

HintHave a real one ready: a guardrail metric moved the wrong way, the win was too small to be worth maintaining, or it came from one segment while hurting the rest.

Brex

Business customers rather than consumers, which changes the statistics. You will not have millions of users to test on, and they want to know what you do about that.

Product senseMedium

Brex has automated matching receipts to card transactions. Which metrics tell you it is working?

  • Separate whether it runs from whether people trust it.
  • Say what a wrong match costs, and how you would count those.
  • Say which metric shows it actually saved the finance team time.

What they're testingMetrics for a feature whose whole value is work that no longer happens.

HintCoverage and accuracy pull against each other - match everything and you match some of it wrongly. Name both, and say which one you would protect.

ExperimentationHard

You are testing something on a narrow slice of customers - startup founders - and there are nowhere near enough of them for a normal A/B test. How would you analyse it?

  • Say what you would do differently from a standard significance test.
  • Say what you would accept as evidence, given the test can never reach power.
  • Say how you stop yourself reading noise as a result.

What they're testingStatistics when the sample size is fixed and small, which is the normal situation at a B2B company.

HintYou cannot go and get more users, so the answer is about what you change instead: the metric, the design, or how certain you claim to be.

Data engineeringHard

How would you make an ETL job idempotent?

What they're testingWhether you have been on call for a pipeline, or only built one.

HintSay what goes wrong without it - a re-run doubling yesterday's numbers - then how you would fix it. Define the word, then earn it.

How to actually use this

  • Pick two from your target company and answer each one out loud, start to finish, before checking anything.
  • Name your metrics before you design the experiment, and say what one row means before you design the schema.
  • Ask what the goal and the constraints are before you start solving. The constraints are usually the real question.
  • For the behavioural one, bring numbers. The follow-up is always "how much".

Pick two and say them out loud

Reading all fifteen won't make you ready. Pick two, answer them out loud, and notice exactly where you stall. Go and read up on that bit, then answer again.

Having the pieces somewhere in your head isn't the same as being able to say them. Saying them is what you're marked on.
